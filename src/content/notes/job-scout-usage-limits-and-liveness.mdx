## What happened

Between September and October, job scout passed aggregator pages and posting addresses that were still rendering as live.<Ref id="E14" /> Dead roles surfaced in the results as live roles.<Ref id="E15" /> In October, the Monday sweep of job scout, a scheduled run on the Opus model, failed with the message "usage limit reached".<Ref id="E12" /> That failure lost a weekly sweep.<Ref id="E13" />

## Causes

1. The Monday sweep was a scheduled run on the Opus model, and it hit a usage limit.<Ref id="E12" /> I read the limit as the reason the sweep did not complete.<Ref id="E12" /><Ref id="E13" />
2. The agent accepted weak evidence that a role was open.<Ref id="E14" /> An aggregator page, or an address that still rendered, passed as proof, and dead roles surfaced.<Ref id="E14" /><Ref id="E15" />
3. I count the second cause as partly a failure of the agent's judgement, not only of the setup around it.<Ref id="E14" /><Ref id="E15" />

## What changed

- Job scout's model was pinned for each task.<Ref id="E25" />
- Its frequent jobs moved to cheaper lanes.<Ref id="E26" />
- Its scheduled runs were spread out.<Ref id="E27" />
- It adopted a liveness rule: a role counts as live only on the employer's own board, or as a full employer-hosted posting within <Fig row="E28">60 days</Fig>.<Ref id="E28" />

## What is still not known

<ul className="unknown">
<li>Which model judged liveness, and whether a more capable one would have rejected the aggregator pages. The record names the model for the Monday sweep only, and nothing here tests another.</li>
<li>How many dead roles surfaced. No count was kept.</li>
<li>Whether the liveness rule held after it was adopted. I recall no recurrence, and nothing measured it.</li>
<li>Whether the rule rejected roles that were live. A stricter rule can drop real ones, and the record does not look.</li>
<li>What the age limit in the rule is measured from.</li>
<li>How much of the usage allowance the sweep needed, and what else used it. The record holds no figures.</li>
<li>Whether the lack of cheaper lanes and of a spread schedule was a cause. Both were adopted; nothing tested either.</li>
<li>Whether the lack of a written definition of live was a cause. The rule was adopted; nothing tested it.</li>
<li>Which of the three changes for usage mattered most. The record lists them together and does not test them one by one.</li>
<li>When the fixes were made. Their rows carry the month, or the first month of the period, of the failure they answer.</li>
</ul>
